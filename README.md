| # | Mechanic | Dynamic | Aesthetic | Bartle type |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| M1 | The character runs forward by itself; swiping left or right changes one of three lanes, swiping up jumps and swiping down rolls. | Players read the obstacles a few metres ahead and pick a lane in a split second, and they learn the train patterns the more they play. | Challenge: speed keeps rising, so every run is a reflex test that gets harder. | Achiever: the aim is a better run on the game's own terms. |
| M2 | Hitting an obstacle head-on makes you stumble and the Inspector catches up; a second hit soon after ends the run. | After a stumble players play very carefully for a few seconds to get clear of the guard. | Challenge: a mistake is punished but not final, so recovery feels tense. | Achiever: failure is a score to beat next time. |
| M3 | Coins sit on the tracks in lines and arcs, and coins carry over between runs to spend in the store. | Players leave the safe lane to follow a coin line, and replay runs just to bank more coins. | Submission: collecting a line of coins is a calm, repeatable habit. | Achiever: a growing coin total is progress. |
| M4 | A hoverboard, started by double tapping, takes the next crash for you and then breaks. | Players save a hoverboard for later in a run and take riskier lanes while it is on. | Challenge: a safety net lets players push for a higher score. | Achiever: it helps you beat the game's limits. |
| M5 | A set of three missions (like collect coins or jump a number of times) raises your score multiplier when completed. | Players deliberately jump or roll more than they need to, to finish a mission, not only to survive. | Challenge: the missions are small targets inside the main goal. | Achiever: missions are a checklist to finish. |
| M6 | New characters and hoverboards with different looks can be bought or unlocked. | Players save up for the look they want and show it in every run. | Expression: you choose how your runner looks. | Achiever: a collection to complete. |
| M7 | A friends leaderboard shows the high scores of other players. | Players replay a run again to pass a friend's score. | Challenge: a friend's score is a target you can beat. | Killer: your higher score pushes someone else down the board. |

**Aesthetic profile:** Challenge leads (M1, M2, M4, M5, M7): speed and one-hit-style failure make each run a test. Submission (M3) and Expression (M6) keep players coming back to collect and customise.

**Player types**
- Primary: Achiever (acting × world), because the score, coins, missions and unlocks (M1, M3, M5, M6) are all targets set by the game itself, and the player acts on them.
- Secondary: Killer (acting × players), because the friends leaderboard (M7) lets a player push another player's score down.

1. [M1] · The catch screen, throwing a ball at a Pokémon found on the map.
2. [M3] · A PokéStop spun to collect free items.
3. [M2] · The eggs screen with the walking distance left before each egg hatches.

| # | Mechanic | Dynamic | Aesthetic | Bartle type |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| M1 | Pokémon appear on a map built from the real world, and you catch one by swiping to throw a ball at it. | Players walk around their area and look at their phone for new spawns, and try curve throws to catch more reliably. | Discovery: you never know which Pokémon is around the next corner. | Explorer: the joy is finding out what the world holds. |
| M2 | Eggs only hatch after you walk a set distance (such as 2, 5 or 10 km). | Players take longer walks, or walk a dog, so their eggs hatch faster. | Submission: ordinary walking becomes a pastime with a reward. | Achiever: each egg is a goal to complete. |
| M3 | Spinning a PokéStop at a real place gives free items such as balls. | Players plan a route that passes several PokéStops, so they do not run out of balls. | Discovery: real places on your route become new things to find. | Explorer: you learn where the stops are. |
| M4 | A Raid is a boss battle at a gym that starts at a set time and is too hard to win alone. | Players gather at the same place at the start time and agree in a group chat who joins. | Fellowship: the boss is beaten together. | Socializer: the group is the reason to be there. |
| M5 | The Pokédex records each species you have caught, with blank slots for the ones you have not. | Players travel to new areas or trade to fill the empty slots. | Challenge: an empty slot is an obstacle to finish. | Achiever: a full list is the target. |
| M6 | Stardust and candy are limited and are spent to power up one Pokémon at a time. | Players pick a few favourite Pokémon and put all their resources into those. | Expression: your team shows what you like. | Achiever: stronger Pokémon are progress. |
| M7 | Friends can send gifts and trade Pokémon with each other. | Players add friends, send gifts every day and swap duplicates they do not need. | Fellowship: the game is shared with friends. | Socializer: friends are the reason to keep playing. |

**Aesthetic profile:** Discovery leads (M1, M3), because the game turns the real world into something to find. Fellowship (M4, M7) and Submission (M2) come next, since raids and trades need other people and walking becomes a daily habit.

**Player types**
- Primary: Explorer (interacting × world), because catching, PokéStop spinning and walking to new places (M1, M3) are all about engaging with the game's world.
- Secondary: Socializer (interacting × players), because raids and trading (M4, M7) give players a reason to meet and talk.
